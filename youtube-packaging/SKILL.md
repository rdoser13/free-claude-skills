# YouTube Packaging & SEO Optimization

Create complete YouTube SEO packages that drive clicks, watch time, and channel growth while maintaining authentic brand voice.

## Priority Order
**Topic > Title > Thumbnail > Hook > Description > Tags**

Packaging comes first. A great video with weak packaging gets buried. Solid packaging with good content wins.

## Workflow Process

When packaging a video, follow this collaborative process:

1. **Analyze the transcript first** — Identify the core content, key proof points, tools mentioned, differentiators, and strongest hook moments before writing anything.
2. **Present title options in a table with character counts** — Always show titles in a table format with a character count column. This makes comparison easy and catches length issues immediately.
3. **Iterate titles collaboratively** — Start with 3-5 options, then refine with the creator. Don't just deliver a final answer. Titles often take multiple rounds.
4. **Match titles to thumbnails** — If thumbnails are provided, recommend specific titles that complement each thumbnail style (see Title-Thumbnail Pairing section).
5. **Build the rest of the package** — Description, tags, hashtags, timestamps, hook analysis.
6. **Verify all video references and prompts** — Re-read the transcript line by line. Every time the creator says "I'll leave a link," "link in the description," or shows a specific prompt on screen, it must appear in the description. Also extract any prompts/commands demonstrated in the video and include them in a 💬 Prompts Used section.
7. **Run the checklist** — Verify everything against the SEO Package Checklist before delivering.

### Title Generation Preferences (Lessons Learned)
- **For tutorial content, default to "How to Use [Tool/Feature]" format** — keyword-forward titles over creative/abstract options. Keep the primary keyword in the first half of the title.
- **Don't over-think parentheticals** — Vivid, specific, and visual parentheticals beat generic ones. "(Army of AI Agents)" > "(AI Agents Working Together)". "(Real World Example)" > "(Step by Step)".
- **Match parenthetical to thumbnail** — If the thumbnail has a strong visual element (e.g., robot army), the parenthetical can reinforce that visual (e.g., "Army of AI Agents").
- **Time-bound titles work for short tutorials** — "in 5 Minutes" or "in 10 Minutes" is a strong hook for videos under 15 minutes. It sets expectations and removes friction.
- **"Switch from X to Y" outperforms "Transfer X Data to Y"** — Action verbs like "switch" and "move" convert better than "transfer" for migration-style tutorials. Prefer "Switch from ChatGPT to Claude" over "Transfer ChatGPT Data to Claude."
- **When the creator provides a title direction, stay close to it** — Don't rewrite from scratch. Refine what they gives you. His instincts on keyword placement are usually right — the parenthetical/hook is where to add creativity.
- **Title iteration is collaborative and multi-round** — the creator picks a favorite from initial options, then asks for more variations of that specific style. When he says "give me more like this," keep the exact same base structure and only vary the parenthetical or a single element. Don't drift from the chosen direction.
- **A creator may want a word from the avoid list when the click value is worth it** — Flag the conflict, offer alternatives, but don't block. His final call. Example: he chose "Game Changer" despite it being on the avoid list because the title felt right for the content.
- **Mix title formats across A/B tests** — Don't default to the same structure for all 3 titles. Creators like to test genuinely different angles. Example: two "How to Build" titles + one "He Built" third-person authority title. Mixing first-person tutorial with third-person proof keeps the A/B test meaningful. **Exception:** when the creator already has a strong base title and is testing a single variable (e.g., "30 Days" vs "Month", with/without "(No Slop)"), keep all 3 variants on that same base. The default is "mix angles," but if the creator provides a base title in his prompt, default to swap-one-element variants instead.
- **Pair specific number + specific time when both are available** — "30 Days of Content in 15 Minutes" beats "30 Days in Minutes" or "Month of Content in 15 Minutes." If the video has both a quantity outcome (30 days, $10K, etc.) and a time investment (15 min, 5 min), use BOTH in the title. Don't strip one to shorten — the specificity is the click.
- **2 titles is often enough for tutorials** — You don't always need 3 A/B options. For straightforward tutorial content, two clean titles (e.g., "How I [X]" + "How to [X]") is the sweet spot. Don't force a third option if the first two cover the angles well. Save 3-title sets for interviews or videos where genuinely different hooks exist.
- **Parentheticals aren't mandatory** — If the base title is clean and keyword-strong under 60 chars, it doesn't need a parenthetical tacked on. the creator often drops parentheticals on tutorial titles where the topic itself is the hook. Only add one if it genuinely strengthens the click.
- **Third-person "He/She Built" works great for interview content** — "He Built a Six Figure Personal Brand That Converts (Copy Him)" outperformed more formulaic options. Third-person framing positions the guest as proof and "(Copy Him/Her)" is a strong action parenthetical.
- **Dollar figures and specificity drive clicks** — Creators consistently gravitate toward titles with specific numbers ($1M, Six Figure, $10K/Month). When proposing titles, always include at least one variation with a concrete dollar amount or metric. Make sure the claim is defensible based on the content.
- **"(AI Proof)" as a parenthetical** — For content about building systems that withstand AI disruption, "(AI Proof)" is a clean, punchy parenthetical Creators like. Works as a modifier on outcome-focused titles.
- **For comparison/review videos, use soft question parentheticals over aggressive ones** — "(Better Than Nano Banana?)" beats "(Nano Banana Killer?)". Question format invites the viewer to watch and decide; "Killer" feels clickbait and the creator consistently picks the softer version. Default to "(Better Than X?)" for head-to-head reviews.
- **Tool roundup videos: pair contrarian parentheticals with year-stamped titles.** "(Most are a Waste)" and "(Stop Wasting Money)" both shipped in A/B for the 2026 AI tools roundup. Pattern: "My Top [Category] in [Year] (Most are a Waste)" — keyword-forward, year for freshness, contrarian parenthetical for click. Don't put a number in the title ("Top 3", "Top 5") unless every thumbnail in the A/B set shows exactly that count — number-thumbnail mismatch hurts CTR.
- **Use models/tools that the video actually demos in the title parenthetical, not bigger/flashier names.** When listing AI models or tools in a parenthetical, match what's said on screen (e.g., "Seedance, Veo, Kling" if those are mentioned, not "Seedance, Veo, Sora" just because Sora is more famous). The title is a contract with the viewer — listing tools that aren't shown in the video creates a bait-and-switch and hurts retention. Pull the parenthetical list directly from the transcript.
- **Add "[Tool] Marketing" long-tail tags for tool roundup videos.** When a video features specific tools used for marketing (e.g., Claude Code, Codex), include "[Tool] Marketing" tags ("Claude Code Marketing", "Codex Marketing"). Captures viewers searching for marketing-specific use cases of those tools.
- **Personal/announcement videos (2026-08-13, AI-anxiety/paternity video):** For an unscripted personal video (announcement, real-talk, behind-the-scenes), the winning A/B mix is: one "Why You..." empathy title, one "[Topic] Is Real (Watch This If...)" direct-address title, one first-person announcement title ("How I Deal With X & [Event] Update"). Empathy parentheticals ("You're Not Alone") beat generic ones ("My Honest Take") — the creator rejected "My Honest Take" as filler via Codex critique. Watch for resolution overclaims on feeling-topics: "How I Stopped/Cured X" promises an outcome an unscripted talk can't deliver; "How I Deal With X" is the honest form the creator picked.
- **The completed curiosity loop is the target pairing:** a "Why do you feel X" question-shaped title + a thumbnail that ANSWERS it ("YOU'RE NOT BEHIND") outperforms matching tone-for-tone. Check pairings for this before defaulting to tone matching, and still never let thumbnail text repeat the paired title.

## Title Guidelines

### Character Limits
- **Target:** Under 60 characters (preferred)
- **Acceptable:** 60-65 characters
- **Maximum:** 70 characters (truncation risk beyond this)
- Always verify and display character count in a table when presenting options

### High-Converting Title Patterns

**Authority + Payoff**
- "I Tested 100+ AI Tools - Here Are the Only 10 Worth Paying For"
- "I've Tested 100+ AI Tools - These 10 Actually Run My Business"

**Contrarian/Skeptical Angle**
- "Is GEO/AEO Worth the Hype? AI Search Expert Reveals the Truth"
- "Watch This Before You Use AI Agents (Ignore The Hype)"
- "Most AI Tools Suck - Here's What Actually Works"

**Outcome-Focused**
- "How to Rank in ChatGPT & Gemini in 2026 (Advice from AI SEO Expert)"
- "He Built a $50K/Month Business With AI Content (Here's How)"

**Curiosity Gap + Specificity**
- "AI Expert Shares His 2026 Playbook (95% Will Fall Behind)"
- "The 2026 AI Playbook: Focus, Scale, or Die"

**Comparison/Versus**
- "Google Gemini vs ChatGPT: Why I Switched (After Testing Both)"
- "5 Reasons Why I'm Using Google Gemini Over ChatGPT"

**Tutorial + Benefit**
- "How to Automate Instagram Posts With AI (High-Quality Images)"
- "This AI Lead Scraping Hack Collects Emails from Google Maps (Free)"

**"How to Use" Keyword-Forward Tutorial (a reliable default for tutorial content)**
- "How to Use Claude Code Agent Teams (Real World Example)"
- "How to Use Claude Code Agent Teams (Army of AI Agents)"
- "How to Use Claude Code Agent Teams in 10 Minutes"
- Keeps the primary keyword front and center for search
- Parenthetical adds the hook/differentiator — keep it vivid and specific, not generic
- Time-bound variants ("in 10 Minutes") work well for shorter tutorials

**"How I" Personal Authority (strong for automation/workflow content)**
- "How I Automated YouTube Videos to Social Media Posts (On Autopilot)"
- "How I Turn One YouTube Video Into Unlimited Social Content"
- "How to Create Claude Code Skills to 10x Your AI Results (Game Changer)"

### Title Elements That Work

| Element | Example | Why It Works |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| Specific numbers | "5 Reasons", "100+ Tools" | Sets clear expectations |
| Year/timeliness | "2026 Playbook" | Signals freshness |
| Parenthetical hooks | "(Don't Fall Behind)" | Creates urgency |
| Personal authority | "I Tested", "Why I'm Using" | Builds trust |
| Contrarian angles | "Most Suck", "Ignore the Hype" | Differentiates from hype |
| Outcome promises | "Here's How", "Worth Paying For" | Clear value proposition |
| Cost transparency | "($200/Month)", "(Free)" | Addresses viewer concerns |
| Action parentheticals | "(Copy Me)", "(On Autopilot)" | Creates urgency + invitation |
| Benefit parentheticals | "(Game Changer)", "(Free Template)" | Reinforces the click reason |

### Proven Parenthetical Hooks
Use these as a reference — pick the one that fits the video's angle:
- **(Free Template)** — When giving away an automation or resource
- **(Copy Me)** — Personal authority + invitation to replicate
- **(On Autopilot)** — Automation content, set-it-and-forget-it angle
- **(Game Changer)** — When the tool/method genuinely shifts how you work
- **(Free)** — Shorter version when space is tight
- **(Step by Step)** — Pure tutorial promise
- **(Here's How)** — Outcome-focused, implies proof
- **(Real World Example)** — Grounds the tutorial in practical application, builds trust
- **(Copy Him/Her)** — Third-person proof + action invite. Strong for interview content where the guest has proven results
- **(AI Proof)** — Positions the system/method as resilient to AI disruption. Clean and punchy for content about building lasting moats
- **(Vivid/Visual Descriptor)** — e.g., "(Army of AI Agents)" — creates a mental image that pairs with thumbnail visuals. Use when thumbnail has a strong visual element worth reinforcing

### Title Presentation Format
Always present titles in a table with character counts:
```
| # | Title | Chars |
|---|-------|-------|
| 1 | How to Repurpose YouTube Videos With AI (Free Template) | 56 |
| 2 | How I Automated YouTube to Social Media Posts (On Autopilot) | 62 |
```

### Title Avoid List
- ALL CAPS (except strategic single words)
- Excessive punctuation (!!!, ???)
- Vague promises ("Life-changing secret")
- Generic templates ("You won't believe...")
- Guest credentials leading (move to description)
- Titles over 70 characters
- "Viral" unless there's actual proof of virality
- Overpromising words that don't match the content

### A/B Testing Strategy

When creating title variations, test distinct hooks:
- **Skeptical/Contrarian** - Appeals to frustrated viewers
- **Educational/Insight** - Appeals to learners
- **Outcome/Tactical** - Appeals to implementers

**Example A/B Set (Interview — SEO/Technical):**
1. "Is GEO/AEO Worth the Hype? AI Search Expert Reveals the Truth" (skeptical)
2. "The New Era of SEO: Optimizing for AI Agents Instead of Humans" (educational)
3. "How to Rank in ChatGPT & Gemini in 2026 (Advice from AI SEO Expert)" (tactical)

**Example A/B Set (Interview — Personal Branding, mixed formats):**
1. "How to Build a $1M Personal Brand That Drives Revenue (AI Proof)" (tutorial/outcome)
2. "How to Build a Personal Brand That Drives $1M in Revenue (AI Proof)" (tutorial/outcome variant)
3. "He Built a Six Figure Personal Brand That Converts (Copy Him)" (third-person proof/action)

**Example A/B Set (Tutorial):**
1. "How to Create Claude Code Skills to 10x Your AI Results" (outcome)
2. "Claude Code Skills Changed How I Use AI (Game Changer)" (personal authority)
3. "3 Ways to Create Claude Code Skills (Stop Using GPTs)" (contrarian)

**Example A/B Set (Automation Tutorial):**
1. "How I Automated YouTube Videos to Social Media Posts (On Autopilot)" (personal authority + automation)
2. "How I Turn One YouTube Video Into Unlimited Social Content" (transformation/outcome)

**Example A/B Set (Feature/Tool Tutorial):**
1. "How to Use Claude Code Agent Teams in 10 Minutes" (time-bound + keyword-forward)
2. "How to Use Claude Code Agent Teams (Real World Example)" (practical proof)
3. "How to Use Claude Code Agent Teams (Army of AI Agents)" (vivid visual + thumbnail pairing)

**Metrics to Track:**
- Click-through rate (CTR) - first 48-72 hours
- Average view duration (most important long-term)
- Audience retention curves
- Subscriber conversion rate
- Comment quality and engagement

## Thumbnail Strategy

### Key Elements
1. **One focal point** - Face, text, or visual (not all three competing)
2. **High contrast** - Bold colors, clean backgrounds (yellow on dark performs well)
3. **Readable text** - Max 3-4 words, huge font
4. **Emotion or intrigue** - Curious/intense expression, striking visual
5. **Legible at small size** - Test at thumbnail scale before publishing

### Text on Thumbnail
- Complements title (doesn't repeat it)
- 3-4 words maximum
- Large, bold, high-contrast font (sans-serif preferred)
- Creates curiosity or states bold claim

### Thumbnail Text Examples

**Bold Claims:**
- "MOST AI TOOLS SUCK"
- "PODCASTS = NEW SEO"
- "$150 AI STACK"

**Benefit Statements:**
- "Here's What Works"
- "100+ TOOLS TESTED"
- "AI ESSENTIALS"

**Contrarian/Fear:**
- "STOP THE AI HYPE"
- "CUT THE AI SLOP"
- "BRANDS ARE TERRIFIED"

**Two-Part Setup → Payoff (white + yellow split):**
- "30 DAYS. 15 MIN" — quantity in white, time in yellow
- Pattern: `[OUTCOME] . [TIME/COST]` with a period as separator
- White word = the promise, yellow word = the surprising payoff
- Pairs perfectly with title lesson "specific number + specific time" — the thumbnail makes the title's two anchors visual
- Use when the video's hook is "big result, tiny investment" (time, cost, or effort)

### Design Notes
- Use high-contrast colors that stand out in search results
- Feature face for personal branding (direct camera contact)
- Bold yellow/white text on dark backgrounds
- Avoid gradients - keep it punchy
- Split-screen format works well for interviews
- Test readability at thumbnail scale (small size) before committing

### Title-Thumbnail Pairing Strategy

**Critical rule:** Title tone must match thumbnail style. Mismatching sends conflicting signals and kills CTR.

**Technical/Tutorial Thumbnail** (shows tool, workflow, automation on screen):
- Pair with "How to" or "How I" titles
- Viewer sees the tool → title confirms "I'm about to learn how to build this"
- Example: Laptop showing Make.com automation → "How I Automated YouTube to Social Media Posts (On Autopilot)"

**Outcome/Transformation Thumbnail** (shows results, platform icons, before/after):
- Pair with proof-driven or transformation titles
- Viewer sees the result → title delivers proof or promise
- Example: YouTube → social platform flow → "How I Turn One YouTube Video Into Unlimited Social Content"

**When A/B testing multiple thumbnails:** Provide separate title recommendations for each thumbnail style. Different thumbnails need different titles to maximize the pairing.

## Hook (First 10 Seconds)

### Hook Patterns That Work

**Cold Open with Best Clip:**
```
[CLIP: Guest saying something provocative]
[CUT TO HOST]
"That's [Guest Name]. [One line of credibility].
And [he/she] just revealed [key insight]. Here's what [they] told me..."
[BEGIN INTERVIEW]
```

**Fast-Cut Teaser:**
```
[RAPID CLIPS - 1-2 seconds each of best moments]
[CUT TO HOST]
"[Guest Name] didn't [achieve X] by accident.
In the next [X] minutes, [they're] breaking down [exact value].
Let's dive in..."
```

**Stat/Data Lead:**
```
[SCREEN TEXT: Provocative statistic]
[HOST ON CAMERA]
"[Stat] because [reason most miss].
[Guest credential]. And [they] just revealed [insight]..."
```

**Direct Promise:**
```
"By the end of this video, you'll know exactly how to [specific outcome].
Plus, I'm giving you [lead magnet] - link is pinned below."
```

**Visual Proof Lead (strongest for automation/tutorial content):**
```
[SHOW the output/result on screen — infographic, social post, dashboard]
"These [outputs] were generated automatically from [source].
And no — this isn't garbage AI slop.
I'm going to show you the exact [automation/system] that does this, step by step.
Plus, I'm giving you [lead magnet] — link's pinned below."
```
Lead with visual proof, then promise, then incentive. Gets all three in under 15 seconds. Avoid opening with a question — statements are stronger than questions for hooks.

### Hook Avoid List
- Long intros about yourself
- "Hey guys, welcome back..."
- Asking for likes/subs before delivering value
- Setting up context before the hook
- Slow rambling setup

## Description Optimization

### First 2 Lines (Critical)
- Appear above "Show More"
- Restate the value proposition
- Include primary keyword naturally

### Standard Description Structure

**1-2 sentences.** Pattern: "This video shows how to [what you'll learn/do — include target keyword AND a brief description of what the video covers]." A second sentence is OK if it reinforces what the viewer will get (e.g., specific outputs, metrics, or deliverables). Keep the second sentence punchy and declarative (e.g., "Free skills for X and Y included.") rather than leading with "You'll also get" or "You'll learn" phrasing.

**IMPORTANT:** The first sentence must mirror the exact keyword phrasing used in the title. If the title says "Switch from ChatGPT to Claude," the description must open with "This video shows how to switch from ChatGPT to Claude..." — not a synonym or rewrite. Pack the keyword into the first sentence. Use a second sentence to expand on the value prop if needed — don't force everything into one sentence if it hurts readability.

**Keep sentence 2 minimal and declarative.** If a single short context sentence works (e.g., "This is OpenAI's new AI image model."), use it. Don't stuff every use case, metric, or deliverable into sentence 2 — that makes the intro dense. One simple framing sentence is often stronger than a packed one. Example: "This video shows how to use ChatGPT Images 2.0 and compares it to Google's Nano Banana for real world use cases. This is OpenAI's new AI image model." — short, clean, does the job.

```
[Sentence 1: "This video shows how to..." — use the EXACT keyword phrasing from the title. Sentence 2 (optional): reinforce what the viewer gets — specific outputs, metrics, or deliverables.]

[🎁 Lead magnet / free resource] - [URL]
[🤫 Paid product or offer] - [URL]
[🚀 Community or newsletter] - [URL]

✅  Related Videos:
- [Related Video 1]: [URL]
- [Related Video 2]: [URL]
- [Related Video 3]: [URL]
- [Related Video 4 (optional)]: [URL]

Use when the video walks through a prompt, automation template, or specific workflow.
Example: For a ChatGPT memory export video, include the exact Claude import prompt word-for-word.
Do NOT summarize — paste the full usable resource so viewers can copy it directly.

**SKIP this section for comparison/review videos.** If the video is a "Tool A vs Tool B" review or a general feature walkthrough (not a specific reproducible workflow), the prompts shown are illustrative rather than templates worth copying. Omit the section entirely rather than padding with prompt snippets that don't stand alone as resources.

**ALSO SKIP when prompts are context-specific to your own setup.** If the prompts shown reference your personal skill names, your Drive URLs, your account names, or any other input the viewer cannot reuse without rewriting, skip the section. The bar is: could a viewer copy-paste this prompt verbatim and get value? If they have to substitute half the words, it's not a template — it's a demo. Demo prompts belong in the video, not the description. **Default to SKIP when the prompt opens with "Use my [X] skill"** — that phrase alone disqualifies it as a reusable template.]

LinkedIn: [your profile URL]
```

### Description Intro Examples (Proven)

**Tutorial intros (use "This video shows how to..."):**
- "This video shows how to scrape LinkedIn profiles with Claude Code using Apify. This LinkedIn scraper can pull post analytics including likes, comments, shares, and more." *(2 sentences — second sentence reinforces what viewer gets)*
- "This video shows how to create Claude Code Skills using three different methods that update in real-time based on your feedback and give you better AI outputs than GPTs or Projects ever could."
- "This video shows how to turn YouTube videos into social media posts automatically across LinkedIn, X, Threads, Instagram, and more using a free automation template."
- "This video shows how to build an AI social media manager with Claude Code that creates and schedules posts across all your social platforms automatically. Free skills for social media management and a dashboard builder included." *(2 sentences — second sentence is punchy and standalone, not "You'll also get...")*
- "This video shows how to use a Claude Code skills hack called Autoresearch that automatically updates your skill markdown files based on your style, context, and workflows."
- "This video shows how to do keyword research in Claude Code using DataForSEO and Ahrefs MCP connections to get high-quality keyword data without spending hundreds on standalone tools."
- "This video shows how to use any AI model inside Claude Code with OpenRouter so you can cross check and get second opinions from GPT, Gemini, and other models without leaving your workflow."
- "This video shows how to create 30 days of social media content in 15 minutes with AI. This process uses Blotato, Claude Code, and a social media skill." *(2 sentences — sentence 2 is a clean tool list, NOT a "you'll learn" pitch. When the workflow uses 2-3 named tools, just list them.)*

**Sentence 2 pattern: "This process uses [Tool A], [Tool B], and [Tool C]."** When the tutorial workflow is built on a specific stack of named tools, the cleanest sentence 2 is just the tool list. Don't pad with what they'll get or why it matters — the title and sentence 1 already covered that. The tool list is informational and helps viewers searching for those tool names.

**Variant: "This process uses [primary tool] to connect with [Tool A], [Tool B], [Tool C], and other [models/tools]."** When the tutorial uses one primary tool to access many secondary tools (e.g., OpenRouter as the gateway to Seedance/Veo/Kling/Sora video models), structure sentence 2 around that connector. Use "and other models" / "and other tools" as the closer, NOT "and more" — cleaner and more specific. Example: "This process uses OpenRouter to connect with Seedance, Veo, Kling, Sora, and other models."

**Personal/announcement intros (use "This video is real talk about..."):** For unscripted personal videos (a feeling-topic, an announcement, a behind-the-scenes check-in), "This video shows how to..." is wrong — there's no tutorial promise to make. Validated 2026-08-13: "This video is real talk about AI anxiety and why you always feel behind in the AI space. I also share a behind-the-scenes look at my setup with Claude Code and Codex, and how I'm heading out on paternity leave for the next few weeks." Sentence 1 still mirrors the chosen title's keyword phrasing; sentence 2 names the other beats of the video. On these videos the creator also trims harder: 2 Related Videos (an evergreen guide + a playlist) is enough, and linking an "obvious" tool like VS Code IS acceptable when the video is a setup tour and the tool is part of the stack being shown.

**Roundup/list video intros (use "This video shows my top...").** For tool roundups, "best of" lists, or stack reveal videos, the pattern is different from tutorials:
- Sentence 1: "This video shows my top [category] in [year]." — declarative, not "how to."
- Sentence 2: "This includes [Tool A], [Tool B], [Tool C], and more." — tool list with "and more" closer when the video covers 4+ tools.
- Example: "This video shows my top AI marketing tools in 2026. This includes Claude Code, Codex, Blotato, Google Gemini, and more."
- Don't force "how to..." on roundup content — viewers aren't there to learn a workflow, they're there for the curated list.

**Interview/podcast intros (use "In this video I interview..."):**
- "In this video I interview [Guest Name] who is a personal branding expert and the owner of [Company]. [Guest] explains how to build a $1M personal brand that drives revenue and leads in an AI-first world. They also break down their exact system for content creation, outbound DMs, and lead conversion that has worked for 50+ founders."

### Video References Section Notes
- Always include your own affiliate/referral tracking params on partner links (e.g., `?ref=yourhandle`)
- If the video has a free template/skill, list the download link first in Video References, pointing at your own lead-magnet landing page
- If the video has a paid product tie-in, add it with 🤫 emoji between the free guide and community links
- For Apify actors, link to the console page (e.g., `https://console.apify.com/actors/[ID]`), not the store page
- **Don't link to obvious/well-known tools** like Claude Code or VS Code Extension — viewers can find those. Focus on the specific tools, integrations, and help docs that are harder to find (e.g. a specific MCP setup guide, GitHub repos for CLIs). **Exception: roundup/list videos.** When the video IS a tool list (e.g., "My Top AI Tools"), include links to every tool featured — even Claude Code, Codex, Gemini — because the list itself is the value prop and viewers expect to click through.
- **Affiliate links go first** in the Video References list. The link with `?ref=` / `?pc=` tracking earns first position; alphabetize or order by relevance after that.
- **Trim references aggressively.** Default to 3-4 references max for short tutorials (under 10 min). Don't link every tool casually mentioned — only the ones central to reproducing the workflow. For an OpenRouter tutorial, that's Blotato (affiliate) + OpenRouter homepage + OpenRouter's specific feature page. Skip Claude Code, skip the model-specific deep links — keep it clean. When in doubt, cut the link.
- **Related Videos: 4 is often enough.** Skill says "5 optional" but the creator typically ships 4. Don't pad to 5 if you don't have a strong fifth — quality over quantity.
- **Don't link tangential/sidebar mentions.** If a tool only appears in an "extracurricular" or "bonus" section of the video (e.g., a quick GPT Image 2 / Codex sidebar in a Blotato tutorial), skip the link. Reference links are for tools central to the workflow being taught — not every tool that flashed on screen. When in doubt: would a viewer following the tutorial actually need this link to reproduce the result? If no, drop it.
- **Include help/setup docs** for MCP tools or integrations shown in the video (e.g., `https://help.blotato.com/api/mcp/setup`), not just the tool homepage.

### AI Rabbit Holes Podcast Format

## Timestamps

### Guidelines
- **Scale to video length — lean toward the low end of each range.** Don't over-index on short videos.

Only split into separate timestamps when a viewer would genuinely want to jump between them.

- Concise, descriptive labels

## Repurposing Strategy

From one YouTube video, create:

2. **Quote cards** - Pull best one-liners for static posts
3. **Email newsletter** - Summarize key points with link to full video
4. **LinkedIn post** - Tease the insight, link to video
5. **Twitter/X thread** - Key takeaways in thread format

### Shorts Opportunities to Identify

When reviewing transcripts, flag clips with:
- Strong opening hooks
- Provocative statements
- Actionable tips
- Memorable quotes
- Surprising insights

## Fail Conditions

- Generic title that blends in
- Title over 70 characters
- Title-thumbnail mismatch (tutorial title on outcome thumbnail or vice versa)
- Cluttered thumbnail with too many elements
- Thumbnail text repeats title exactly
- Slow hook that loses attention or opens with a question instead of a statement
- Hook buries the proof point (if one exists, it should be in the first 5 seconds)
- No lead magnet mention in description
- Tags exceed 500 characters
- Tags in lowercase instead of Title Case
- Missing "How To" long-tail tag variation
- Missing timestamps
- Adding a dedicated lead magnet timestamp (the creator doesn't want these)
- No clear CTA or next step
- Can't repurpose into shorter content
- Description missing key reference links
- Description missing prompts/commands shown in the video (should be in 💬 section)
- Video references not verified against transcript (every "link in the description" mention must be accounted for)
- Affiliate links missing tracking params
- Description intro longer than 2 sentences for tutorials (2-3 sentences OK for interviews)
